Should a Nairobi boda boda rider switch from petrol to electric?

Not yet, on daily profit. Electric riders earn KES 837 a day against KES 996 on petrol because they drive 75 km against 85. Per kilometre the two are nearly level.

120 riders (9 electric, more than 100 petrol), Nairobi, 2023 study. GPS trip records and daily financial surveys. Amounts in Kenya shillings (KES).

The decision this supports A rider should switch to electric only if their routes let them cover about 89 km a day with a swap station on the way; below that, petrol still earns more. Per kilometre the two are nearly level (KES 11.18 electric against 11.68 petrol), electric costs 40% less for energy and 81% less to maintain, and the KES 159 daily gap comes from electric riders driving 75 km against 85. The data cannot say why electric riders drive less, and only 9 electric motorcycles were tracked, so treat the 89 km break-even as directional. Operators should share swap-station and charging records for the tracked electric riders before the next fleet purchase, so the cause can be found.
