Do electric motorcycles pay better than petrol for Nairobi boda boda riders?

Electric riders earn KES 837 a day against KES 996 for petrol riders. Per kilometre they are almost level, so the gap is about distance, not cost.

Violet Mutuku, data analyst. 120 GPS-tracked riders, Nairobi Motorcycle Transit Comparison Dataset (Mendeley Data, 2023).

The question

E-mobility operators want to know if electrifying a fleet makes sense for the rider. Electric bikes cost more upfront, so the question is whether day-to-day economics can justify the premium, and what drives any gap.

The answer

Projected profit is distance multiplied by electric profit per kilometre (KES 11.18). The orange line is the petrol average of KES 996. It assumes profit per kilometre stays constant as distance grows.

How I got there

I compared daily profit, cost per kilometre and distance for electric and petrol riders over the same period, to separate cost, revenue and utilisation as causes of the gap.

Financial figures came from daily phone surveys, and some were inconsistent, such as zero revenue on a 90 km trip. I corrected these records using fuel-consumption benchmarks for petrol and published battery-swap pricing for electric, rather than removing them, so the sample size stayed intact.

Electric energy cost per kilometre is 40% lower and maintenance 81% lower. Electric keeps 73.8% of revenue as profit against 59.9% for petrol. Electric riders also have a safer floor: a worst day of KES 50, against a loss of KES 8,800 for petrol.

What I'd do next

  • Work on distance first. Costs are already near parity, so more subsidy on energy or maintenance adds little. Closing the 10 km gap to 85 km a day is the larger lever.
  • Find out why electric riders drive less: range, swap-station access, route assignment or learning curve. Each needs a different fix.
  • Compare fleets on profit per kilometre, not daily profit, because daily profit depends on how much a rider chooses to drive.

Caveats

Only 9 electric motorcycles were tracked against more than 100 petrol ones, so treat the findings as directional. Revenue and costs are survey-based and partly corrected by estimate. A larger, longer electric cohort is needed before a large fleet decision.
